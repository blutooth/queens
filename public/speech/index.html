Address by Her Royal Majesty Obonganwan Marie Erete, Queen Aruk II

Business Dialogue: Africa–UK Trade, Investment, Enterprise and Sustainable Development
11th Annual London Political Summit & Awards · Institute of Directors, 116 Pall Mall · October 2026

Your Excellencies, Presidents of sovereign Republics; Your Excellencies, Governors of States; Right Honourable Speakers; Honourable Members of Parliament; Your Excellencies, High Commissioners and Ambassadors; the distinguished Summit Chair, Professor Kishan Devani; Royal Fathers and Royal Mothers; captains of industry; distinguished ladies and gentlemen.

I bring you royal greetings from the ancient Kingdom of Eniong Abatim in Efik land, and from the queens of Africa, in whose name I am privileged to speak.

I also convey the apologies of His Royal Highness Oba (Dr.) Amb. Olusegun Idowu Aderemi, JP, the Summit Royal Father of the Day, who is unable to be with us today but hopes to join us tomorrow.

Permit me first to bow in honour of our Global Grand Patron, the late Right Honourable Raila Amolo Odinga. He taught a continent that peace is the soil in which prosperity grows, and that trade, wisely pursued, is diplomacy by other means. We gather in his memory, and we must labour in his spirit.

Excellencies, the relationship between Africa and the United Kingdom is old, deep and, at times, complicated. It is rich in treaties, communiqués and handshakes. Yet history will not judge us by the warmth of our handshakes. It will judge us by the bread on our people’s tables, the factories in our towns, and the futures we open for our young. The task before this dialogue is therefore clear: to convert political goodwill into economic partnership, and declarations into dividends.

That conversion requires capital, yes, but above all it requires trust. And here I come to a resource that Africa has too long left in the treasury unspent: the leadership of her queens.

Long before the modern state, Africa’s queens were custodians of land, guardians of markets, arbiters of peace and keepers of communal wealth. Today, across the continent, royal women hold the confidence of communities that no investor can reach by contract alone. Where a queen gives her blessing, land disputes are settled, cooperatives flourish, and a project becomes the people’s own. Sustainable development is not built only in ministries and boardrooms. It is built in the palaces and villages where trust has been earned over centuries.

It was this conviction that inspired the African Global Queens Summit, which I had the honour to convene here in the United Kingdom this August. Its Masterclass, The Transformative Leadership Experience, was held at the University of Oxford. There, queens and women cultural leaders from Africa and the diaspora resolved to equip themselves for leadership in the unification and progress of our continent.

But resolve alone is not enough. Inclusion must be designed, not merely desired. I therefore respectfully commend four changes to this assembly:

First, give traditional institutions, and queens in particular, a formal seat at the table in Africa–UK trade and investment frameworks, so that community consent is built in from the start, not sought as an afterthought.

Second, open dedicated channels of finance and enterprise support to women-led and community enterprises, including those championed by royal houses.

Third, invest in capacity: in governance, negotiation, digital trade and sustainable enterprise for queens, women and young Africans, so that partnership is between equals.

Fourth, place African queens in positions of real authority, on development boards, investment councils and peace initiatives, where their counsel can shape outcomes and not merely adorn ceremonies.

Excellencies, Africa does not come to this table with a begging bowl. She comes with a basket full of promise: youthful energy, boundless resources and wisdom tested by time. Let the United Kingdom come as a partner, not a patron. And let us together build a prosperity that is shared, sustainable and worthy of the generations who will inherit it.

May peace guide our trade, and may trade secure our peace.

I thank you.

— Queen Aruk II · Founder and Convener, African Global Queens Summit —
