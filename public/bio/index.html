Obonganwan Barrister Marie Erete

QUEEN ARUK II · MBONGA MO MISOH I · ADAHA AND OBUKOK OF EKOT ETONKO · ADA IDAHA KE EFIK EBURUTU

Obonganwan Barrister Marie Ikpeme Anwakang Erete, Queen Aruk II, hails from an illustrious royal lineage deeply rooted in the legacy of Queen Aruk I. She is the youngest and cherished daughter of Prince and Princess Okon James Ikpeme Ikpeme Aruk Anwankang Ikpeme Erete, a family renowned for its enduring tradition of leadership, service and integrity. Through her father's line she descends directly from Ete Anwakañ, remembered as Obong Anwakang, and his consort Aruk, the first female regnant queen of the Eniong Kingdom, in whose honour the Aruk stool was founded.

Raised within the rich cultural heritage of her ancestral homeland, Obonganwan Barr. Marie Erete was immersed from an early age in the customs, values and responsibilities of royalty. While firmly grounded in tradition, her vision extended far beyond her immediate environment. She began her professional journey as a news presenter in Calabar before relocating to London to pursue higher education and professional advancement.

Law and Professional Life

In the United Kingdom she distinguished herself academically, earning degrees in Business Methods (BHD) and Law (BA Hons), alongside several professional qualifications. Her commitment to excellence culminated in her becoming a Solicitor Advocate with Higher Rights of Audience, a rare and prestigious achievement that underscored her legal expertise and determination.

Her professional career spanned reputable financial institutions and prominent legal firms. Notably, she founded and successfully managed Chamberlin Solicitors for over a decade, earning recognition for her leadership, commercial acumen and ethical practice. Yet, beyond professional success, she was driven by a profound desire to serve humanity.

Humanitarian Service

Guided by an enduring spirit of altruism, Obonganwan Barr. Marie Erete returned to Nigeria, where she dedicated herself to humanitarian service. Her efforts transformed lives and communities, earning her widespread admiration and numerous accolades for her compassion and commitment to social justice. In 2021 the Eniong Abatim Community presented her with the Abatim Ambassador Award for outstanding contributions to education in the Eniong Abatim Nation.

Royal Office

In 2018, the Bukong Asang community unanimously selected her to ascend the revered throne as Queen Aruk II. Embracing this sacred responsibility with humility and resolve, she accepted her role as a custodian of tradition and a catalyst for progress. Her coronation symbolised both continuity and renewal within the Ikpeme Erete lineage.

Her investiture and coronation were endorsed on 15th September 2018 by representatives of the Ikpeme Royal Families, through the Ikpeme Family Union, and confirmed on 15th January 2019 by the Village Headship Forum of the Ikpeme Family Union, in its collective resolve to reconstitute and sustain the Royal Stool of Queen Aruk I. In 2019 the heads of royal families, houses and clans of Calabar North West, representing Eniong Abatim, Ito, Ukwa, Eki and Idere, endorsed her installation as Queen Aruk II for Calabar North West. She was enthroned at Ekot Etonko. Her life and work are recorded in Life and Times of Obonganwan Aruk II: A Historical Research and Documentation for Preservation and Posterity, published in the Citizen Outlook Newspaper Public Education Series in 2019.

On 19th July 2024 the Council of Efut Nation installed her as Mbonga Mo Misoh of the Efut Nation. She also holds the titles Adaha and Obukok of Ekot Etonko and Ada Idaha ke Efik Eburutu.

Royalty and Philanthropy

As Queen Aruk II, she seamlessly integrated royal leadership with philanthropy. Through initiatives such as medical outreaches, poverty alleviation programmes and women's empowerment projects, she addressed critical social challenges. The Queen Aruk II Foundation emerged as a beacon of hope, supporting marginalised and disadvantaged populations across Southern Cross River State.

In honour of her late mother, she also established the Mary Bassey Erete Foundation (MBEF), further expanding her humanitarian mission. Through this platform, she championed access to education, healthcare and social protection for the most vulnerable. Her work continues through the Queen Aruk II International Court of Peace, Royalty Preservation and Humanitarian Services, and in the United Kingdom through ARUK II Humanitarian Services (UK) C.I.C.

The African Global Queens Summit

Queen Aruk II is the founder and convener of the African Global Queens Summit, also known as the African Queens and Women Cultural Leaders Summit, a Pan-African initiative she conceived following her installation as a regnant queen in 2018. The 2026 Summit took place in the United Kingdom from 14th to 31st August 2026, bringing together queens, royal women and women cultural leaders from Africa and the diaspora. Its centrepiece was the Summit Masterclass, The Transformative Leadership Experience, held at the Mathematical Institute, University of Oxford, on 17th and 18th August 2026.

The Summit was established to build the capacity of Africa's queens and women cultural leaders, and to position them for leadership in the unification and progress of the continent. For generations, Africa's royal women have been the keepers of memory, custom and community. The Summit equips them with the knowledge, networks and confidence to carry that stewardship onto the wider stage of governance, diplomacy, enterprise and peace-building. Its conviction is simple and enduring: a united and prosperous Africa will be built not only in parliaments and markets, but also in the palaces and communities where its queens have always led.

Through the Summit, Queen Aruk II brings together the preservation of African royal and cultural heritage and its service to women, children and communities today. Her ceremonial dress reflects the same purpose: she wears the Efik Ọnyọnyọ, made from local fabrics, carrying both the history of the form and the materials of her own land.
