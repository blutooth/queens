Meet the Four Most Powerful Queens of Africa

They are custodians of culture, guardians of community, and voices for the continent's future. This August, four of Africa's most formidable royal women convene in the United Kingdom for the African Global Queens Summit. Meet the queens.

I

Queen Aruk II

Nigeria · Convener of the Summit

A regnant queen and traditional ruler of Eniong Abatim in Cross River State, Obonganwan Barr. Marie Erete, Queen Aruk II is the convener of the African Queens Summit — an event she conceived, organises and funds entirely herself.

A formidable Solicitor Advocate with Higher Rights of Audience and a fearless champion of human rights, culture and tradition, she has pushed humanitarian service to its limits. Through the Queen Aruk II Foundation and the Mary Bassey Erete Foundation, she funds medical outreaches, empowers women economically, helps many set up small businesses, pays children's school fees, and helps farmers grow their enterprises.

Most recently she donated 50 fishing boats to communities across the region — after gifting 80 boats to her Eniong community the previous year — among countless other acts of philanthropy.

II

Queen Diambi

Democratic Republic of Congo

HRM Queen Diambi Kabatusuila Tshiyoyo Muata is the traditional queen of the Bakwa Luntu people of Central Kasaï, in the historic Luba Kingdom. Born in Belgium and raised between Kinshasa and Europe, she was a university valedictorian and earned advanced degrees in psychology and public administration, working as a psychotherapist and professor.

She discovered her royal lineage in 2016 and was crowned in 2017; she was later honoured as Queen Mother of the Bantu People of Brazil. She founded the Elikia Hope Foundation, which serves over 40,000 homeless children in Kinshasa, and is a global voice for African identity, unity and restitution.

IV

Queen Best Kemigisa

Uganda · Tooro Kingdom

HRH Queen Best Kemigisa Akiiki is the Queen Mother (Omujwera Nyina Omukama) of the Tooro Kingdom in western Uganda. Widowed in 1995, she served as regent and guardian to her son, Oyo Nyimba Kabamba Iguru Rukidi IV — crowned at just three years old, a Guinness-record youngest reigning monarch.

She is founder of the Toro Women's Development Association and the Queen Mother Best Foundation, and co-founder of the African Queens and Women Cultural Leaders Network — devoting her reign to empowering women, youth and marginalised communities.
